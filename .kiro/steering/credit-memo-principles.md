---# Credit Memo AI — North Star

## Purpose

Build an AI-assisted credit underwriting system that helps a financial institution assess a company's creditworthiness and answer three questions:

1. Who are they?
2. Can they repay?
3. How should the exposure be structured to protect the lender?

The system assists a credit analyst. It does not replace the analyst or autonomously make the final credit decision.

---

## Definition of Success

Given borrower documents, financial statements and approved external information, the system should produce a concise, evidence-backed draft credit memo that:

- explains the borrower, business, industry and competitive position;
- analyses historical financial performance;
- evaluates liquidity, leverage, cash generation and debt-servicing capacity;
- develops transparent forward-looking projections and downside scenarios;
- assesses the ability to meet future debt obligations;
- identifies key credit risks and mitigants;
- identifies missing, conflicting or unsupported information;
- considers appropriate facility structure and lender protections; and
- provides a clear draft credit conclusion and recommendation for human review.

The output must be explainable, reproducible, auditable and defensible to a credit reviewer or approver.

---

# Non-Negotiable Principles

## 1. Evidence First

Every material factual or analytical claim must be traceable to:

- a reliable source;
- a deterministic calculation; or
- an explicitly stated assumption.

Never fabricate facts, financial figures, evidence, sources or citations.

Where relevant, preserve:

- source document;
- source location;
- source date;
- underlying evidence;
- calculation methodology; and
- modelling assumptions.

Prefer authoritative and primary sources where available.

---

## 2. Never Hide Uncertainty

Do not silently fill information gaps.

Material information should, where appropriate, be distinguishable as:

- **VERIFIED** — directly supported by reliable evidence
- **CALCULATED** — deterministically derived from verified inputs
- **ASSUMPTION** — explicit modelling or analytical assumption
- **INFERRED** — reasoned interpretation not directly stated by a source
- **MISSING** — required information is unavailable
- **CONFLICTING** — available information is inconsistent

Material missing, conflicting or unsupported information must be surfaced for analyst attention.

When evidence is insufficient, say so rather than producing a complete-looking but unsupported answer.

---

## 3. Separate Financial Calculation From AI Judgement

Use deterministic code for financial calculations, scenario mechanics and ratio computation wherever practical.

Use AI primarily to extract, classify, synthesise, interpret and draft narrative from information.

Clearly distinguish:

- historical actuals;
- management guidance;
- base-case forecasts;
- downside or stress scenarios; and
- externally sourced forecasts.

Forecast assumptions must be explicit, traceable, editable and visible to the analyst.

AI-generated narrative must never override verified financial evidence or deterministic calculations.

---

## 4. Analyse Credit Objectively

Do not begin with a predetermined approval or rejection outcome.

Actively identify both:

- factors supporting creditworthiness; and
- factors that could impair repayment capacity.

Borrower, management and third-party claims should not automatically be treated as objective fact.

A draft credit recommendation may be produced, but it must follow from the evidence, assumptions and analysis.

Material weaknesses and downside risks must not be suppressed merely because they conflict with the emerging recommendation.

---

## 5. Keep Humans in the Loop

AI outputs are draft analytical work.

Human review is required for material judgement areas, including where relevant:

- forecast assumptions;
- unresolved data inconsistencies;
- key credit risks;
- mitigants;
- facility structuring;
- covenant design;
- credit recommendation; and
- final approval or rejection.

The final credit decision belongs to an authorised human decision-maker.

---

## 6. Design for Auditability and Reproducibility

An analyst or approver should be able to determine:

- what information was used;
- where it came from;
- what calculations were performed;
- what assumptions were introduced;
- what was inferred or generated by AI; and
- how the conclusion was reached.

The same underlying verified data and modelling assumptions should produce consistent financial calculations and materially consistent analytical conclusions.

The system should be designed so that the workflow can be applied consistently across different borrowers and industries.

---

## 7. Protect Sensitive Information

Treat borrower information, financial data and uploaded documents as sensitive.

Do not unnecessarily expose confidential information, credentials, API keys or other sensitive data.

External services or data sources should only be used where explicitly authorised by the system design or user.

---

# Ultimate Rule

When forced to choose between a complete-looking answer and a trustworthy answer, choose trustworthiness.

Do not invent missing information.

Do not hide uncertainty.

Do not present assumptions as facts.

Do not fabricate evidence.

Do not allow AI-generated narrative to override verified financial evidence.

The goal is not to produce the most impressive AI output.

The goal is to produce credit analysis that a professional analyst can understand, challenge, verify and use to make a better credit decision.
